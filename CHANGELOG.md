- Star Clawds was **Clawd Wars**: [history/star-clawds.md](history/star-clawds.md) (up to 2.28.3)
- Marvelous Clawds was **Avengers: Clawd Wars**: [history/marvelous-clawds.md](history/marvelous-clawds.md) (0.1.0)
- Detective Clawds was **Justice League: Clawd Wars**: [history/detective-clawds.md](history/detective-clawds.md) (0.1.0)

## 4.1.1 (2026-10-25)

### Added
- **A session waiting on a background job says so.** When an AI leaves something running in the background (a render, a build, a dev server) and ends its turn, it used to show as idle, then nodded off, though it would carry on by itself the moment the job finished. Now it stands by with a teal hourglass, the sand running down, and its card reads "WAITING ON A JOB", with the job by name ("Waiting on: Render the full promo"); the screen saver and its ticker say the same. The first thing it does once the job has woken it ends the wait. Which AIs: Claude Code (Bash in the background, and Monitor), Qwen Code (a background shell command, and its monitor), Copilot CLI (an async shell command) and Gemini CLI (a background shell command, only with `tools.shell.backgroundCompletionBehavior` set to inject or notify and `experimental.modelSteering` on, since otherwise it never wakes by itself). Not Codex, which checks on its running commands itself rather than being woken, or pi, which has no background jobs. Background agents were already covered: they're on stage, and the session waits on them. A job that never wakes its session stops counting after an hour. The menu's Status Symbols lists the hourglass as "Waiting on a job".

## 4.1.0 (2026-10-25)

### Changed
- **Version numbers climb slower.** A new universe now raises the middle number (the next, Superclawdtural, will be 4.1.0, not 5.0.0), and everything else, features included, the last one (4.1.1). The first number stays at 4 and only moves by hand, for a big enough change to the app as a whole. Under the rule 4.0.0 started, every universe raised the first number, which would have reached 13 with the universes already planned. `scripts/next-version.sh` and the guide's "Version numbers" follow the new rule; `./release.sh` works the number out as before.

- **A new kind of magic, the grip** (`Magic(grip: true)`), for telekinesis that holds rather than throws: there's no stream; the caster's clenched hand glows, a faint thread wavers from it, and whoever they're fighting is hauled a few pixels off the ground, kicking, while a glowing coil squeezes in tight round their middle, then dropped for a breath before the next. - **A character a habit moves takes its name tag and speech bubble along** (`StageQuirks.jitter`). Each character on stage now also says how big its art pixels are (`OnStage.scale`), so a habit can work out how far that is. Requested for Superclawdtural's Clawdstiel, who has no sense of personal space.

### Fixed
- **The promo's title lands again, and GitHub's thumbnail shows it.** Each universe sends its legend tumbling down the vortex, and Superclawdtural's made a sixth wave, which pushed CLAWD CLASH's landing past the end of the shot: it only arrived as the terminals wiped in, and the video's first frame (the thumbnail GitHub shows), still set to a fixed time, caught it as a speck in the vortex. The waves now close up to fit however many universes there are, so the title always lands at 11.8 s, and the thumbnail is taken just after it lands.
- **The promo's music no longer plays two tunes at once at Clawd Royale.** The groove ran straight through the Royale shot while the teaser's own fanfare, in another key, came in over it. Now the groove keeps going to the end card and Royale brings no music of its own: the emblems and the CLAWD ROYALE title are slapped on like stickers, with a smack each. The groove's lead tune bows out at the slam and stays out, so nothing lands on the FWIP like a second fanfare and the tune doesn't start over for the last few seconds. The Royale teaser keeps its fanfare.
- **Stranger Clawds' BMX bikes keep their wheels.** A ride that drives in was always drawn on a ship's canvas, whatever height it asked for, so the bikes, drawn two rows taller to keep Dustin's cap clear of the top, lost their bottom two rows, the tyres and the ground under them, as they came and went. Rides that drive in now get their own height, as the TARDIS always did.
- **The screen saver's ticker says when it was an agent.** An agent leaves the scene when its job is done, so "Dustin is done: Fix the tests" named someone you could no longer see. It now reads "Dustin, Eleven's agent, finished and left: Fix the tests", and an agent arriving "joined Eleven as an agent". In the screen saver, Full Screen and the window's screen-saver view.

## 3.7.1 (2026-10-19)

### Added
- **Choose which universes you use** (Settings → Universes). Every universe ships with the app, and each now has a checkbox, listed in two columns under a bold Universes heading. Uncheck one and it leaves the Universe menu, Random, Clawd Royale's cast and places, and the screen saver's choices, and the hook stops casting new characters from it; check it again and it's back exactly as it was. If the universe on show is unchecked, the app moves to the first one still in, as if you'd picked it; in Clawd Royale a place from it gives way to another, and sessions already cast from it keep their characters until they end. At least one universe stays in. It's saved as the list of what's removed, so a universe that arrives with an update shows up checked.
- **Stormtroupers never hit anything.** To keep the joke going, the Stormtrouper's and the Scoot Trooper's bolts never land: in a shoot-out, a shot that would have hit sails high and wide past the target's shoulder instead, and the target doesn't even flinch. Everything else about the fight is as before (their shots still get blocked, dodged, deflected and shot down), and the Dearth Troopers, the elite, still hit. It's a persona flag, `missesEverything`, so any universe can have its own hopeless marksman.
- **Star Clawds changes places with more of the films' wipes.** It had two, an edge sweeping left to right and a clock hand, taking turns. Now there are seven, a different one from last time each change: the straight edge sweeping right, left, down or up, a slanted edge sweeping corner to corner (as in The Empire Strikes Back), and the clock hand turning either way. Every one has a soft edge, as the films' wipes do: the old place fades into the new across a band along the edge (behind the clock hand, a slice of its turn), where it used to cut hard. In the window, the screen saver and the window's screen-saver view, and in the trailers' Star Clawds wipes.
- **Each universe is one folder now** (the universe-modules refactor). Every universe now keeps its facts (`<Prefix>Facts.swift`) and everything the engine asks of it (`<Prefix>Universe.swift`, a `UniverseModule`) in its own folder, with a `universe.conf`; `Tools/universes.sh` builds the list of universes from the folders, so the app, the screen saver, the hook and the tools find them there instead of in hand-kept lists. The Transitions tool asks each universe for its trips. The README pictures' desktop line-ups and social previews are each universe's own (`module.docs`), and the showcase pictures live in each universe's `Promo/<Prefix>Showcase.swift`, drawn by RenderShowcase's kit and found through a generated list. RenderPromo is split the same way: its shared parts are a kit (`Tools/RenderPromo/Kit/`), each universe's trailer lives in its folder as `Promo/<Prefix>Reel.swift` (a `PromoReel`, which also says who's in its desktop brawl and its window), and `main.swift` keeps only the reels that mix universes: the promo, the bloopers and the Clawd Royale teaser. Every step renders the same pictures, byte for byte, as before it. `Tools/CheckUniverse/check.sh <Folder>` (or `--all`) checks a universe before it's handed off and prints a checklist: its manifest against `NUMBERS`, no template leftovers, file and top-level names carrying its prefix, counts, homes, place keys, magic looks, signatures that are really drawn, nemeses, rides, transitions, every saved id against `main`'s, that no code outside its folder names it, the builds, and sheets to look at in `build/check/<key>/`. A new universe starts from the template, `Sources/App/Universes/_Template/`: a whole universe as small as one can be (one hero, one villain, HEM, one place, one kind of trip, a trailer with the shared shots), with every rule written next to the code it applies to; `Tools/new-universe.sh <Folder> <Prefix> <key> "<Title>" <number>` copies it and renames it into the universe's own. The template itself is never shown in the app: no normal build compiles it, a copy left unrenamed stops the build with a plain message, the app and `build.sh --release` refuse it, `release.sh` checks the built app's `--list-universes`, `TEMPLATE=1 ./build.sh` runs it from `build/` without saving anything about it, and `check-universe --template` (which `release.sh` runs too) makes sure it still builds and draws. The clawdify guide (and `CONTRIBUTING.md`) now follows the folders: a contributor gets a number from `NUMBERS`, starts with `Tools/new-universe.sh`, grows only their folder through the module's members, asks the owner for an engine hook instead of editing the core, and hands off once `check.sh` passes and the new "When a universe is complete" checklist is ticked, after which the owner does the changelog, README, `llms.txt`, docs pictures, cask and release. Every shipped universe now passes that check too: each file and every top-level name that isn't `private` starts with the universe's prefix (`SWHero` is `StarClawdsHero`, `cbLine` is `clawdbergLine`, Marvelous' `Art/Heroes1.swift` is `MarvelousHeroes1.swift`, most helpers simply became `private`), no universe calls another's helpers any more, `scripts/pr-guard.py` reads numbers, prefixes and cast sizes from the manifests and facts files, and `build/preview` makes a name two universes share (`sandman`) an error you settle with the key in front (`dc:sandman`), all with every saved id and every pixel unchanged.

The cantina's band, otherwise random, is pinned in the tools for it (`starClawdsBandForced`). The first step of making universes modules.

- **A guard for pull requests** (`scripts/pr-guard.py`, run on every PR by `.github/workflows/pr-guard.yml`). A universe is Swift compiled into the app, so a malicious one could use the app's Accessibility grant or rewrite the AIs' hooks. The guard reads a PR with git as plain text, never building or running it, and:
  - **sorts every changed file** into green (a universe's own folder, the docs' pictures, README, CHANGELOG), yellow (passes, listed for the maintainer to read closely: registration files and the rest of the app) and red (fails: build and release scripts, dotfiles such as `.github/` and `.claude/`, instructions for AI assistants, the hook and the code compiled into it, `HookInstaller`, `SaverInstaller`, `Jump`, `main.swift`, new core files, symlinks, executables);
  - **checks the Swift lines a PR adds to a universe folder**: no file, process, network, other-app, environment or app-internal APIs (including a call hidden in a string's `\( )`, or a backticked name), only the four imports drawing needs, no attributes such as `@_silgen_name`, no `#if` or macros, no `main.swift`;
  - **blocks shadowing**: a new non-private top-level name must start with the universe's prefix and must not reuse a name from the app's own code, and extensions are allowed only on `Scenes`, `Location`, `Cast` and the universe's own types, with no added conformances. That stops a one-line `func chmod` in an art file from silently switching off the state folder's permission fix;
  - **refuses hidden code**: invisible and text-direction Unicode, non-ASCII outside strings and comments, code after a long run of spaces, lines over 260 characters, universe files over 2,000 lines;
  - **checks cast ids**: no universe has more villains than the hero ids leave room for, or more heroes than fit before the next universe's ids. This replaces the cast-size `assert`, which release builds compile out.

  CI uses `pull_request_target`, so it's always main's copy of the guard that runs, and a PR can't edit the guard to pass itself. It has a read-only token and no secrets. Run it locally with `python3 scripts/pr-guard.py <PR number>` or `python3 scripts/pr-guard.py main <branch>`. Tested against Clawd of the Rings' PR #3, where it flags the hook-side `StatusStore.swift` and `llms.txt` changes and seven unprefixed names, and against a fake malicious universe, where all 24 planted tricks were caught.
- **`.github/CODEOWNERS`** names the maintainer on every path, for main's ruleset to require that review.
- **`CONTRIBUTING.md`**: what a pull request may change and why. It lists the guard's green, yellow and red files, the rules for universe code (APIs, imports, prefixed names, extensions, layout, ids) and how to run the guard on your own branch. A section for the maintainer covers reviewing a PR: read the guard's report, never start an AI assistant or plain `./build.sh` in an unreviewed checkout, and try it with `./build.sh --untrusted`. The README's Contributing section links to it, the clawdify guide says to prefix or privatise top-level names, puts driving rides in `Ride.drives`, and says which files to leave to the maintainer when sending a PR from a fork, and `llms.txt` describes the guard.

## 3.5.1 (2026-10-16)

### Added
- **`./build.sh --untrusted`, for trying someone's pull request.** It builds into `build/` only and signs ad hoc, so the copy gets none of the permissions you've given the installed app (Accessibility, Automation). It doesn't install it, launch it, or touch any AI's hooks: read the changes, then open `build/Clawd Clash.app` yourself. Plain `./build.sh` installs, rewrites hooks and launches with your Accessibility grant, which is the wrong thing to do with code you haven't reviewed. In the README's build section and `llms.txt`.

### Changed
- **Every build has the hardened runtime**, not only releases. Local builds (`./build.sh`, `--no-install`, `--untrusted`) are signed with `--options runtime` and the Apple Events entitlement, so another program running as you can't load its own code into the app with `DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES` and use the app's Accessibility grant. Click-to-jump works as before.
- **Apple silicon only.** Clawd Clash is built for Apple silicon (arm64) alone from now on; 3.5.0 was the last release for Intel Macs. Release builds no longer compile everything twice and glue the halves together with `lipo`, so `./release.sh` takes about half as long, and the zip is smaller. The Homebrew casks now say `depends_on arch: :arm64`, so on an Intel Mac brew refuses the upgrade with a clear message instead of installing an app that can't open; Intel users keep 3.5.0. The README's requirements, `llms.txt` and the clawdify guide just say Apple silicon, without mentioning Intel.

### Fixed
- **A release can no longer go out un-notarized by accident.** `release.sh` used to treat any `notarytool` failure, a network blip or a locked keychain included, as "no profile", and ship without notarization; the cask then clears the quarantine flag, so brew users would have skipped Gatekeeper without anyone noticing. Now a missing profile stops the release unless you pass `--no-notarize`, and a profile that's there but fails always stops it. The check runs before the build, so it fails in seconds, not after one.
- **`release.sh` is harder to break halfway.**
  - The version must be `X.Y.Z` (it goes into the tag, the cask, the plists and a regex).
  - `gh` must be signed in before anything starts.
  - "Not found" and "couldn't check" are told apart for the tag and the release, so a network error no longer reads as "absent".
  - The app's signature is verified, and when notarized the stapled ticket and Gatekeeper's verdict too, before anything is published.
  - With `--force`, the release's zip and notes are swapped in place instead of the release being deleted first, so the cask's download URL never points at nothing.
  - An existing tap that fails to clone stops the release, instead of an empty repo being pushed in its place.
  - Old releases are pruned last, after the cask push, sorted by version rather than date, so re-releasing an older version never prunes the newest.
  - The tap's temporary folder is removed even when the script fails.
- **`brew uninstall --zap` also removes the screen saver's options**, kept in the screen saver host's container (`Clawd Clash Saver/options.json`).
- **Your prompts are no longer readable by other accounts on the Mac.** The hook keeps clipped copies of your prompts, the AI's replies, session titles and the start of each command in `~/.claude/clawd-clash/state.json`, and that folder and its files were created with default permissions (0755 and 0644), so another user account on the same Mac could read them, though Claude Code keeps the originals in an owner-only folder. `StatusStore.withLock` now sets the folder to 0700 on every write (tightening existing installs on their next hook event) and writes `state.json`, `state.json.corrupt` and `state.lock` as 0600. The hook, the app and the screen saver all run as you, so nothing else changes. Found by a Claude Security scan.
- **`--jump` and `--ax-dump` are left out of normal builds.** These click-to-jump diagnostics ran with the app's Accessibility and Automation permissions for whatever launched the app, so any program running as you could start Clawd Clash with them and read the window and tab titles of other apps. They are now compiled only into a `./build.sh --dev-tools` build, which is off by default and refused together with `--release`; plain `./build.sh`, `--no-install` and releases don't answer them. Click-to-jump itself is unchanged. Found by a Claude Security scan.
- **A `--dev-tools` build can no longer be installed or released.** `./build.sh --dev-tools` used to be refused only with `--release`, so a plain `./build.sh --dev-tools` installed the diagnostics build into /Applications, where it kept the app's Accessibility grant. It now has to go with `--no-install`. Its two reports start with "Clawd Clash dev-tools build, never ship this one", and `release.sh` stops if that text is anywhere in the release binary. `Jump.dump`, which only `--ax-dump` uses, is compiled only into those builds too.
- **Connecting an AI can no longer wipe its settings file.** `HookInstaller` read any settings file it couldn't open (one left root-owned by `sudo claude`, say) as empty, then wrote its hooks over it, losing everything else in the file: permissions, model, plugins. Now only a missing file counts as empty; any other read error stops the install for that AI and says which file. The backup it makes before its first change (`.before-vader`, `.before-clawd-clash`) used to fail silently; now a failed backup stops the change too.
- **Disconnecting no longer removes hooks that aren't ours.** A hook counted as Clawd Clash's whenever its command merely contained `clawd-clash-hook` (or an old app's hook name), so your own `my-clawd-clash-hook-wrapper` would be removed with ours. Now the command has to run a file with exactly that name.
- **Everything Clawd Clash writes is yours alone.** The hook and the app now run with `umask 077`, so every file they create is owner-only: the state, the screen saver's feed (`screensaver.json`), and the AIs' settings files they rewrite. The feed's folder in Application Support is also tightened to 0700 once per launch, for folders made 0755 by earlier versions. The feed holds the same prompts and project names as `state.json`; it was only covered by `~/Library` being owner-only.
- **The hook reads at most 16 MB of an event, and at most 1 MB of a `.git/config`.** An event bigger than that (a huge tool response) is skipped rather than held in memory, and an oversized git config is treated as no remote. Both run on every event, inside every AI session.
- **A Homebrew upgrade no longer fails when the old app can't run.** brew's uninstall step runs the installed app with `--uninstall-hooks`; when macOS refused to run that copy ("Apple could not verify…") and killed it, the whole upgrade stopped with "terminated by uncaught signal KILL". The casks now mark that step `must_succeed: false` (`release.sh`), so the upgrade carries on and the hooks just stay, pointing at the path the new app is installed to. The four casks in the tap are already patched, but brew uninstalls with the cask file saved at install time, so this protects installs made from now on; an install from before can still hit it once, and deleting `/Applications/Clawd Clash.app` and running `brew install --cask --force clawd-clash` gets past it.

### Fixed
- **Qwen Code and Gemini CLI hook hints name the right hook again**: they said to enable `clawd-wars`, but the hook is called `clawd-clash`.

- **A build run from source replacing your installed screen saver.** A copy of Clawd Clash run from `build/` (`./build.sh --no-install`) swapped the screen saver in `~/Library/Screen Savers` for its own build, as an app update would. It now leaves it alone, as it already left the AIs' hooks.
- **The screen saver's Options only ever keeping the first pick.** After installing, the universe chosen in Options stuck, and later picks were ignored until the screen saver was removed and installed again. macOS keeps the screen-saver host running between uses, and on recent versions a setting saved through ScreenSaverDefaults by one of its processes isn't seen by another that's already running. Options are now saved in a small file of the screen saver's own (`Clawd Clash Saver/options.json` in the host's Application Support), read afresh each time, so a new pick shows within a second.
- **Two Star Wars blasters firing the wrong shot.** Every Star Wars gun fired the same red bolt. Sebulba now fires the flame jet he used on Anakin from his pod racer, and the Jawa's ion blaster fires a blue ion bolt, like the one that drops R2 on Tatooine.

- **A clean build from source**: `./build.sh` no longer warns about an unused value in the Deadpool fourth-wall transition (`ComicWipes.swift`).
- **The screen saver showing the wrong universe, or flicking between both.** The universe is now picked in one place, the screen saver's own Options: the app's Settings menu had its own pick, which always won, so choosing Marvel in Options still showed Star Wars. - **The screen saver vanishing from System Settings' list.** On macOS 14 and later, the list of third-party screen savers comes from a background process (WallpaperLegacyExtension) that remembers what it found. A saver removed and put back while that process ran (a reinstall, an update, a removal) stayed missing from the list, although it was installed in the right place (`~/Library/Screen Savers`).
